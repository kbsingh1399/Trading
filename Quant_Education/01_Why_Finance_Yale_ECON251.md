# Yale University ECON 251: Financial Theory
## Lecture 1: "Why Finance?" — Master Educational Notes
**Instructor:** Prof. John Geanakoplos (James L. Frank Professor of Economics, Yale University; Co-Founder, Ellington Capital Management)

---

### Executive Overview
In this lecture, Professor John Geanakoplos elevates finance into a foundational mathematical discipline of economic science. Blending pure theory with 15 years running Ellington Capital Management, Geanakoplos proves that asset prices are forward-looking aggregators of private information, while exposing the structural flaws of the Efficient Markets Hypothesis (EMH). Real-world crises are driven by **The Leverage Cycle**—the systemic expansion and sudden contraction of collateral haircuts and margin requirements.

---

### Core Conceptual Modules Covered:
1. **Academic Evolution:** From vocational subject to scientific dominance; Irving Fisher, J. Willard Gibbs, James Tobin, and Cowles Foundation.
2. **The Three Paradigms:**
   - *Efficient Markets Hypothesis (EMH):* No-arbitrage valuation; assumes complete markets and frictionless borrowing.
   - *Behavioral Finance (Robert Shiller):* "Irrational Exuberance", psychological fads, social narratives.
   - *The Leverage Cycle (John Geanakoplos):* Incomplete markets, collateral constraints, dual-price equilibrium (Interest Rate AND Margin Requirement).
3. **The Anatomy of a Crisis:** How collateral requirements fall to 3% during euphoria (33:1 leverage), and spike to 70% during uncertainty, triggering forced fire sales and systemic crashes.
4. **Market Microstructure:** The ABX Subprime Index crashed from 100 to 60 in April 2007, sounding the alarm 10 full months before the Dow Jones peaked in October 2007.
5. **Hedge Fund Case Studies:** Kidder Peabody collapse (1994), LTCM blowup (1998), Ellington Capital margin calls, and the Warren Buffett proposition.
6. **The 10 Quantitative Puzzles:**
   - Lottery discounting (100M USD annuity vs 40M USD cash lump sum).
   - Professor tenure consumption smoothing over 40 years.
   - University endowment deficit management (Yale 5B USD loss: amortized permanent income vs mass firing).
   - Bookmaker dynamic delta hedging in baseball World Series.
   - Red/Black card game: Why you must keep drawing even when odds are unfavorable (convex stopping option).
   - 15-year vs 30-year mortgage prepayment option evaluation.
   - Mortgage pool burnout and adverse selection.
   - Principal-guaranteed structured notes (Zero-coupon Treasury + Leveraged Alpha).
   - Binary AIDS cure biotech vs GE valuation.
   - Momentum vs mean-reversion in asset returns.
7. **The 16-Student Double Auction Experiment:** How decentralized open-outcry trading discovers clearing prices (20-25 USD) and transfers goods to highest-valuation agents in 120 seconds.
8. **Macroeconomic Limits:** Pecuniary externalities in collateral constraints and the Overlapping Generations (OLG) mechanics of Social Security.
