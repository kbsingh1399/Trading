# 3. EMPIRICAL FINDINGS: PAIRS TRADING DYNAMICS ACROSS ASSET CLASSES

### Empirical Findings:
1. The Mean-Reversion Trap in Macro Spreads:
   Across 211k 15m bars, naive mean reversion on spreads (fading Z >= 1.80) produces net negative returns across crypto, forex, and CFDs because high spread Z-scores are predominantly driven by structural macro catalysts or idiosyncratic token runs. Fading the move without volume absorption or half-life filtering acts as a losing counter-trend trade.
2. The Power of Spread Momentum in Crypto:
   In high-beta crypto assets, spread momentum (going long the outperformer and short the underperformer when |Momentum| >= 1.5 Std) generates substantial positive returns:
   - DOGEUSDT / LINKUSDT: +202.89% Net PnL (452 trades, 45.1% Win Rate, Beta: 0.798)
   - DOGEUSDT / BCHUSDT: +219.67% Net PnL (415 trades, 48.0% Win Rate, Beta: 0.734)
   - DOGEUSDT / ADAUSDT: +151.54% Net PnL (470 trades, 43.4% Win Rate, Beta: 1.044)
   - SOLUSDT / BCHUSDT: +85.72% Net PnL (800 trades, 47.0% Win Rate, Beta: 0.928)
   - SOLUSDT / ETHUSDT: +44.75% Net PnL (929 trades, 47.9% Win Rate, Beta: 2.145)
3. The Disjoint Graph Matching Requirement:
   When multiple pairs share common legs (e.g. DOGE in DOGE/LINK, DOGE/BCH, and DOGE/ADA), an idiosyncratic shock in DOGE triggers simultaneous stop-outs across all pairs, causing catastrophic drawdown spikes (up to 28% max DD). Restricting the portfolio to a maximum weighted matching of disjoint pairs (e.g. DOGE/LINK, SOL/ETH, XRP/BCH) decouples individual token exposure and compresses portfolio variance.
