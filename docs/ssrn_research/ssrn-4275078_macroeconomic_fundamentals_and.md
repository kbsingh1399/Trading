Abstract
Based on asset pricing theory, we posit and ﬁnd that equity markets and
cryptocurrency markets share a common fundamental. Our cointegration tests
show that the most important asset pricing primitive, consumption, can serve
as the common fundamental. We further show that additional macroeconomic
factors, as well as uncertainty and sentiment, all play a role in explaining
the deviation from fundamentals. To understand the linkage between equity
markets, cryptocurrency markets, and the macroeconomy, we suggest the
following three channels: (i) portfolio allocation decisions, (ii) intermarket
order ﬂows, and (iii) technological adaption expectations.

1.
Introduction
The novelty and volatility of cryptocurrency prices have led policymakers and
investors to adopt the belief that its price dynamics are determined purely by specula-
tive behavior from irrational agents.1 This has spurred the ﬁntech literature’s eﬀorts
in trying to document stylized facts and theoretically understand the mechanisms
that drive these empirical regularities. One of the key facts that has seemingly been
uncovered is the lack of connection between equity markets and cryptocurrency
markets. However, this is puzzling from an asset pricing perspective that predicts
commonality between diﬀerent markets.
We resolve this puzzle by using cointegration tests to show that cryptocurrency
prices are indeed determined by economic fundamentals and investigate the role that
the macroeconomy may have in driving the price process of cryptocurrencies. In
contrast to conventional wisdom, our results show that cryptocurrency prices and
equity prices are cointegrated and determined by common fundamentals. We ﬁnd
that cryptocurrency price contains a more transitory component reﬂecting the high
volatility of cryptocurrencies compared to equity prices. The deviation from these
fundamentals is not only due to speculative sentiment but also macroeconomic factors
and time-varying uncertainty. These results rule out models where cryptocurrency
prices are solely driven by "irrational exuberance".
Our study is the ﬁrst, to our knowledge, in taking this approach to establish
the long-run equilibrium relation between prices of equities, cryptocurrencies, and
macroeconomic fundamentals, and examine whether macroeconomic fundamentals
explain the ﬂuctuations of the cryptocurrency market. Our methods and results
complement Bhambhwani, Delikouras, and Korniotis (2019), who examine cointe-
gration from a micro-perspective. We empirically investigate whether a long-run
equilibrium exists between equity and cryptocurrency prices and if macroeconomic
fundamentals drive the long-run equilibrium by taking a theoretically grounded
empirical approach.
The theoretical motivation for a common long-run fundamental is relatively
straightforward. The present value model implies that price is cointegrated with
its fundamentals if the fundamental growth rate is stationary (see, e.g., Campbell
& Shiller, 1987; Engel & West, 2005). However, the lack of consensus on cryp-
tocurrency fundamentals makes it diﬃcult to empirically identify. We circumvent
this impediment by exploiting a nascent development in the asset pricing literature
showing that return variation across asset classes can be explained by a common
risk factor (e.g., Cochrane, 2011; Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen, & Vrugt, 2018;

To address whether macroeconomic factors play a role in equilibrium, we take a
two-fold approach. In our ﬁrst approach, from the perspective of consumption-based
asset pricing models, we consider an economy in which aggregate wealth consists
of two asset classes: equities and cryptocurrencies. Motivated by Campbell and
Mankiw (1989) and Lettau and Ludvigson (2001a), we focus on the representative
agent’s budget constraint and derive a cointegration relation between aggregate
consumption, equities, and cryptocurrencies. This allows us to directly test for a
common fundamental among these three variables. Our results strongly support
the existence of a long-run equilibrium relation and indicate that consumption is
able to serve as a common fundamental between equity and cryptocurrency prices.
Our results are also consistent with the international ﬁnance literature that links
currency returns to consumption growth (e.g., Burnside, 2012; Lustig, Roussanov,
& Verdelhan, 2011).
In our second approach we investigate the determinants of deviations from the
long-run equilibrium since the deviation predicts expected future cryptocurrency
returns. Explaining the source of equilibrium deviations will help us understand
the economic contents of cryptocurrency price ﬂuctuations. We use asset pricing
theory to help guide the set of candidate state variables: the consumption-wealth
ratio, GDP growth, unemployment rate, uncertainty, and investor sentiment. Our
empirical results show that macroeconomic fundamentals, uncertainty, and investor
sentiment are important factors that inﬂuence cryptocurrency prices.
To further understand the long-run equilibrium relationship, we explore three
plausible mechanisms linking the equity market, cryptocurrency market, and the
macroeconomy. Firstly, the state of the economy jointly aﬀects wealth, consumption,
and investment decisions.
Therefore, if investors hold cryptocurrency in their
portfolios, their allocation decisions will jointly aﬀect cryptocurrency and equity
prices through demand changes driven by economic ﬂuctuations. Secondly, there
could be inter-market order ﬂow in and out of the stock market and the cryptocurrency
marketrelatedto the business cycle, analogizing from Hau andRey (2006)’s exchange
rate model. Finally, economic growth and technological innovations positively
aﬀect expectations of future technological adaptations and induce commonality (and
therefore cointegration) between cryptocurrency and equity prices.

2.
The Long-run Relation Between Equity Price,
Cryptocurrency Price, and Fundamentals
Asset pricing theory illustrates that the key macroeconomic variables aﬀecting
the investment opportunity set should be priced factors in equilibrium (e.g., Breeden,
1979; Merton, 1973). Lettau et al., 2014 show that the cross-sectional variation in
returns across asset classes can be captured by a common risk factor. The empirical
evidence consistently suggests that the prices of equity, bond, and foreign currency
appear to vary with macroeconomic variables (e.g., Engel & West, 2005; Fama &
French, 1989; J. H. Stock & Watson, 1999). However, the prior literature has not
found any evidence of correlation between cryptocurrency prices, equity prices, and
macroeconomic variables.
To analyze the joint behavior of equity prices, cryptocurrency prices, and macroe-
conomic variables, we present a framework based on the budget constraint of a
representative economy in which aggregate wealth is assumed to contains two types
of assets: equity and cryptocurrency. Our framework is motivated by Campbell and
Mankiw (1989) and Lettau and Ludvigson (2001a).

4.1.
Features of the Data
We want to test the relationship between equity prices, cryptocurrency prices,
and the macroeconomy. Therefore, we extract daily data for the S&P 500 price series
from CRSP and the Bitcoin price series from CoinMarketCap as representative
proxies of their respective markets and aggregate to a monthly series by using the
end-of-month prices. We use data from Coinmetrics.io to calculate the market-
cap weighted average of the ten largest cryptocurrencies as a proxy for the overall
cryptocurrency market in our robustness tests. The sample consists of monthly
data with a sample period of July 2010 until December 2021. We log transform
and standardize the price series in order to ease interpretation and minimize matrix
invertibility issues given the large magnitudes in diﬀerences between their prices
(Kaufmann & Juselius, 2013).
Our macroeconomic variables come from a variety of sources. We use Markit’s
monthly GDP index. The data on cay are available at a quarterly frequency from
Lettau’s website and is available until 2019Q3. Following Guo and Whitelaw (2006),
we interpolate the data into a monthly frequency. We use monthly personal con-
sumption expenditure data from the US Bureau of Economic Analysis downloaded
from FRED. Monthly unemployment rates and CPI-U from the US Bureau of Labor
statistics are also downloaded from FRED. Our data on uncertainty come from
Jurado, Ludvigson, and Ng (2015), Ludvigson, Ma, and Ng (2019), and S. R. Baker,
Bloom, and Davis (2016). We also use the change in the CBOE Volatility Index to
proxy for expected time-varying risk. The data on investor sentiment comes from
two sources. The ﬁrst source is a sentiment measure that has been orthogonalized
with respect to macroeconomic measures (M. Baker & Wurgler, 2006) and runs until
December 2018. The second source of sentiment data is the University of Michigan
Survey of Consumer Sentiment accessed from FRED. All variables are standardized.

4.3.
Understanding Deviations from the Equilibrium
Having established evidence that there exists a cointegration between consump-
tion, equity price, and Bitcoin price and that the cointegration error predicts cryp-
tocurrency returns, in this section we turn to explaining the cointegration error,
i.e., the deviation from the long-run equilibrium. The deviation from equilibrium,
termed cab, may be due to agents in these markets being driven by speculative
sentiment or due to a rational time-varying risk factors explanation. In order to
disentangle the importance of non-rational and rational explanations, we consider
three potential sources associated with the deviation: investor sentiment, uncertainty,
and macroeconomic fundamentals.
The literature shows that investor sentiment may contain a market-wide compo-
nent that is likely to inﬂuence prices on many securities in the same direction at
the same time (e.g., Barberis, Shleifer, & Vishny, 1998; Shiller, 2000) Therefore,
we use the investor sentiment index from M. Baker and Wurgler (2006) and the
University of Michigan Consumer sentiment survey as proxies for sentiment.
It is well known that uncertainty is associated with business cycle and rises
sharply in recessions (e.g., Bansal & Yaron, 2004; Bloom, 2009; Jurado et al., 2015;
Ludvigson et al., 2019). We use monthly data on time-varying macroeconomic
uncertainty from Jurado et al. (2015) and Ludvigson et al. (2019). Macroeconomic
uncertainty is estimated at three forecast horizons: 1 month, 3 months, and 12 months.
We run a principal components analysis on these diﬀerent forecast horizons to reduce
the dimensionality of the data while capturing the time-varying uncertainty. We
also consider the economic policy uncertainty measure of S. R. Baker et al. (2016).7

6.
Discussion
The results from the previous section show that (i) there exists a common
fundamental between equity and cryptocurrency price and (ii) the deviations from
the equilibrium are relatedto macroeconomic fundamentals, uncertainty, andinvestor
sentiment.
These two results that have been established have important implications. Firstly,
these ﬁndings imply that the price of cryptocurrencies itself depends on macroeco-
nomic fundamentals and uncertainty. This key ﬁnding overturns the conventional
wisdom that views cryptocurrency prices as being unmoored from the economy or
depending solely on sentiment. Secondly, we provide evidence that cryptocurrency
price is related to fundamental macroeconomic variables – cay and unemployment –
as well as VIX, economic policy uncertainty, and sentiment. That is, these macroe-
conomic variables are all plausible candidates for the cryptocurrency fundamental
in addition to the monetary fundamentals (store of value, fungibility, scarcity, etc.)
and technological fundamentals (decentralized network, blockchain, etc.) that the
12


### Page 15

literature has focused on. Therefore, investors can use this additional information
when making portfolio allocation decisions.
We propose the following mutually non-exclusive plausible channels to explain
the linkages between the macroeconomy, Bitcoin prices, and equity prices. First, the
framework we use to motivate our trivariate tests provides one possible explanation.
The state of the economy jointly aﬀects wealth, consumption, and investment deci-
sions. Therefore, if investors hold cryptocurrency in their portfolios, their allocation
decisions will jointly aﬀect Bitcoin and equity prices through demand changes driven
by the business cycle. This explanation is related to studies trying to understand the
commonality across asset classes. (e.g., Cochrane, 2011; Koijen et al., 2018; Lettau
et al., 2014). This joint allocation decision would induce comovement between
equities, consumption, and cryptocurrencies.
The secondmechanism is in a similarspiritofinternationalportfolio choices. Hau
and Rey (2006) show that exchange rates, equity prices, and equity portfolio ﬂows are
jointly determined under incomplete foreign exchange risk trading in an equilibrium
model. This model in our setting would imply higher returns in equity market relative
to the cryptocurrency market are associated with US dollar depreciation. Net equity
ﬂows into the cryptocurrency market are positively correlated with a cryptocurrency
appreciation. These dynamics generate positive correlation between Bitcoin price
and equity price.
For example, when an agent purchases a good denominated in BTC, he initiates
the purchase of BTC for USD. This implies there is a straightforward correspondence
between net capital inﬂows and outﬂows between the US economy and Bitcoin
economy. Aggregate wealth covaries with the business cycle, causing the stock
price to positively covary with business cycle. If the US economy is in a state of
expansion, net inﬂows into the Bitcoin economy will increase causing appreciation
in BTC resulting in a positive correlation between the equity markets and the crypto
market. While somewhat controversial at present, this order ﬂow channel will grow
in importance as the Bitcoin economy (the buying and selling of goods using Bitcoin)
develops and grows.
A further possible mechanism is the interaction between the expectation of
technological adaption and economic growth (Beaudry & Portier, 2006; Galor &
Tsiddon, 1997; Pástor & Veronesi, 2009). This channel shows that prices incorporate
forward-looking behaviors (rational and irrational). We argue that economic growth
is related to future technological adaptations and induces cointegration between
Bitcoin and equity prices.
7.
Conclusion
We empiricallyestablishthatan equilibrium relationshipexists between the equity
market and cryptocurrency market. A simple model is formulated based on prior
asset pricing theory where both markets are linked through common state variables.
We then show that cryptocurrency price ﬂuctuations are not due solely to speculative
13


### Page 16

sentiment, but also due to uncertainty and the state of the macroeconomy. These
results are not only interesting from an academic perspective, but also contributes to
the current contentious debate among regulators about how similar cryptocurrencies
are to other assets and securities. This model is empirically supported and vindicated
by the recent turmoil in equity and crypto markets in April and May of 2022. For
example, the IMF notes that cryptocurrencies, unlike other ﬁnancial assets "[have]
their value depends entirely on self-fulﬁlling expectations" (IMF, 2019, p. 25). Our
results are evidence that these virtual assets, like other more traditional assets, obey
asset pricing theory and behave in similar ways to ﬁnancial assets.
